## 1. Introduction to Organizational Behavior
* **Organization**: A consciously coordinated social unit, composed of two or more people, that functions on a relatively continuous basis to achieve a common goal or set of goals.
* **Behavior**: An action or a reaction caused by either known or unknown stimuli.
* **Course Objectives**: Understand basic concepts/theories, examine how individual, group, and structural variables interact to create knowledge for prediction and control, and appreciate the implications in the context of Indian situations and research.

## 2. Classical Management Approaches

### A. Bureaucracy (Weber)
A component of formal organization that uses rules and hierarchical ranking to achieve efficiency.
* **Ideal Type**: A method of investigation/model for evaluating specific cases. It may not actually exist perfectly in reality, but it is expected or desirable.
* **Five Basic Characteristics**:
  1. **Division of Labor**: Specialized experts perform specific tasks. 
     * *Drawbacks*: Leads to fragmentation of work, isolates workers, lessens job security, and leads to **Alienation**. Relative isolation creates **Trained Incapacity**—the tendency of workers to become so specialized that they develop blind spots and fail to notice potential problems.
  2. **Hierarchy of Authority**: Each position is under the supervision of a higher authority. Traced by identifying who is answerable to whom.
  3. **Written Rules and Regulations**: Emphasizes clear standards for adequate performance and ensures organizational continuity.
     * *Drawbacks*: Overemphasis leads to **Goal Displacement**—over-zealous conformity to official regulations instead of focusing on the actual goal.
  4. **Impersonality**: Officials perform duties without giving personal consideration to individuals. 
     * *Drawbacks*: Contributes to frustration and disaffection.
  5. **Employment based on Technical Qualifications**: Hiring based on skills rather than favoritism; performance measured against standards.
     * *Drawbacks*: Dysfunctionality exists via the **Peter Principle**—every employee within a hierarchy tends to rise to their level of incompetence.
* **Parkinson’s Law** (Cyril Northcote Parkinson, 1955): 
  * "Work expands so as to fill the time available for its completion."
  * People finish tasks just in time for the deadline even if they could finish earlier (e.g., an elderly lady taking all day to write a single postcard).
  * In organizations, a pyramid grows because managers add artificial layers to introduce hierarchy, promote people, and keep them motivated.
  * Bureaucrats create work for other bureaucrats, and managers hire multiple subordinates so neither competes with the manager.
  * *Diminishing returns*: Extra time/effort initially works, but eventually does not lead to increased accuracy. Used as a severe criticism against bureaucratic inefficiencies.

### B. Scientific Management (F.W. Taylor)
* Advocated working in a highly systematic manner.
* **Key Principles**:
  * Scientific analysis of all tasks for maximum efficiency.
  * Managers design jobs to achieve maximum technical division of labor through advanced job fragmentation.
  * Separate planning (done by management) from execution (done by workers).
  * Select the best man, instruct him extensively, and use a **"carrot-and-stick" approach** (incentive payments) to persuade people to try harder.
  * Maintain a "minimum interaction model" between manager and worker.
* **Assessment**: Treats individuals as isolated units viewed apart from their group. The worker is deemed merely an instrument in the production process, not a human being. Assumes the natural instinct of men is to take it easy.

### C. Fordism (Henry Ford)
* Initiated mass production, splitting the production process into tiny, highly standardized parts.
* Worker was reduced to a machine, could not use discretion, simply followed orders, but was compensated with high wages.
* **Assessment**: Term coined by Antonio Gramsci (1936). It was a hegemonic form of industrial organization where capitalists dominated workers through control over wages and the labor process.

### D. Alienation (Karl Marx)
A radical, revolutionary theory critiquing how the capitalist mode of production and extreme division of labor destroys the human spirit.
* **The Core Idea**: Workers feel like strangers in their own world—isolated, unwanted, and powerless. Instead of work being an expression of human creativity, it becomes a dehumanizing trap for survival.
* **Four Forms of Alienation**:
  1. **Alienation from the Product**: The worker has no control over the end product; it is owned entirely by the employer/capitalist.
  2. **Alienation from the Process of Work**: The worker is merely a "cog in the machine." Work is imposed, forced, and provides no intrinsic satisfaction—only extrinsic satisfaction (wage).
  3. **Alienation from Other Workers**: Instead of colleagues cooperating, the capitalist system pits workers against each other as rivals/competitors for jobs, bonuses, and promotions.
  4. **Alienation from Human Nature (Self)**: Man differs from animals through creative, conscious intelligence. Capitalism denies this true essence, turning humans into commodities and leaving them unfulfilled and spiritually homeless.
* **Reification & Fetishism**: Capitalism turns human labor into a "thing" (commodity fetishism), creating an illusion that market forces are natural, uncontrollable powers (reification), when in reality, they are controlled by the capitalist class to exploit labor.

## 3. The Human Relations Approach
Proposed by **Elton Mayo**, this approach directly challenged Scientific Management.
* **Core Philosophy**: A worker's behavior is attributed to their **'sentiments'** rather than just economic reason. Workers are thinking individuals whose ideas are shaped by co-worker interactions, not just instruments of production.
* The individual is more loyal to the informal group and governed by group norms.
* Consultation regarding hours, rest, and conditions is necessary. The factory performs both economic AND social tasks. Maintaining human dignity is paramount.

### A. The Hawthorne Effect (Illumination Experiment)
* Two groups of female employees (test room vs. control group) were studied to see the impact of lighting on productivity.
* **Result**: Productivity increased in both rooms, even when lighting was lowered to near darkness. 
* **Conclusion**: Productivity increased because of the motivational effect of being observed and receiving friendly supervision, proving human/social factors matter more than physical conditions.

### B. The Bank Wiring Observation Room (BWOR)
* A group of 14 men (wirers, solderers, inspectors) were observed performing a repetitive, tiring task.
* **Findings**:
  * The group developed their own codes of conduct and social norms enforced by social pressures.
  * They decided 6,000 units was a "fair day's work" and maintained a steady flow, deliberately choosing NOT to maximize output (fearing the company would lower the base pay rate).
  * Frictions arose if individuals deviated: Underperformers were abused and called **‘chisellers’**; overperformers were called **‘ratebusters’**.
  * Frictions existed between cliques (front-room high-status vs back-room low-status).
* **Conclusion**: It is not sufficient to just design jobs and payment systems (as Taylor suggested). People interact in unpredictable, complex ways in informal groups that override classical formal systems.

## 4. Personality Traits Influencing OB

### A. The Big Five Model (OCEAN)
1. **Openness to Experience**: Range of interests, fascination with novelty. Imaginative, creative, artistic, sensitive, intellectual.
2. **Conscientiousness**: Measure of reliability. Responsible, dependable, persistent, organized, hardworking.
3. **Extraversion**: Comfort with relationships. Sociable, gregarious, assertive, talkative, outgoing.
4. **Agreeableness**: Propensity to defer to others. Good-natured, cooperative, warm, caring, trusting.
5. **Emotional Stability**: Ability to manage/control emotions. Calm, self-confident, secure (Positive) vs. nervous, depressed, insecure (Negative).

### B. Other Major Traits
* **Self-Evaluation (Self-perspective)**: The degree to which people like or dislike themselves. Positive core self-evaluation means feeling effective, capable, and in control. Negative means disliking oneself and feeling powerless.
* **Locus of Control**: 
  * *Internals*: Believe they control what happens to them.
  * *Externals*: Believe what happens is controlled by outside forces (luck, chance).
* **Machiavellianism (Mach)**: Pragmatic, maintains emotional distance, believes "ends justify the means." High Machs manipulate more, win more, and are great at bargaining/negotiations.
* **Self-Monitoring (SM)**: Ability to adjust behavior to external/situational factors. High SM are highly sensitive to situational cues, act like "chameleons", progress faster in careers, and get promoted more.
* **Risk-Taking**: 
  * *High Risk*: Make quicker decisions with less info (good for entrepreneurs). 
  * *Low Risk*: Slower, need more info (good for large stable organizations).
* **Type A vs. Type B**:
  * *Type A*: Always moving/in haste, impatient, multitaskers, obsessed with numbers and acquiring things.
  * *Type B*: Never suffer time urgency, play for fun, can relax without guilt, no need to display achievements.

### C. Introversion vs. Extraversion (Energy Sources)
* **Introversion**: Energy from inner world. Quiet energy, listen more than talk, think quietly inside head, think then act, comfortable alone, work behind-the-scenes, focus on one thing.
* **Extraversion**: Energy from outside world. High energy, talk more than listen, think out loud, act then think, like being around people, prefer public roles, multitask, easily distracted.

## 5. Emotions and Psychoanalysis

### A. Emotional Intelligence (EI)
The extent to which people are self-aware, can manage emotions, motivate themselves, express empathy, and possess social skills.
1. **Self-Awareness**: Capacity to be aware of how you are feeling.
2. **Managing Emotions**: Capacity to balance anxiety, fear, and anger so they don't interfere with tasks.
3. **Motivating Oneself**: Ability to remain optimistic and strive despite feedback, barriers, and failures.
4. **Empathy**: Ability to understand how others are feeling without being explicitly told.
5. **Social Skills**: Ability to get along with others and establish positive relationships.

### B. Narcissism
* From Greek myth of Narcissus (vain, loved his own image). 
* High self-importance, excessive admiration, entitlement, arrogant. 
* Selfish, exploitative, treat others as inferiors, talk down to those who threaten them.

### C. Sigmund Freud's Psychoanalytic Theory
Three levels of personality that maintain a dynamic equilibrium:
1. **Id**: Instinct-based, present since birth. Unconscious and free. Mantra: **Pleasure**, avoiding pain, ignoring consequences (greed, hedonism).
2. **Ego**: Practical, rational, logical. Operates on the **Reality** principle. Maintains perfect balance between Id and Superego for a balanced personality.
3. **Superego**: Highest **Moral** and ethical values of society, religion, or faith.

## 6. Stress Management

* **Definition**: A dynamic condition where an individual is confronted with an opportunity, demand, or resource related to what they desire, and the outcome is perceived to be both **uncertain** and **important**.
* **Types of Stressors**:
  * **Challenge Stressors**: Workload, time pressure, job responsibility (often positive/motivating).
  * **Hindrance Stressors**: Role ambiguity, conflict, red tape (negative obstacles).
* **Sources of Stress**:
  1. **Environmental**: Uncertainty is the biggest cause (Economic, Political, Legal, Technological).
  2. **Organizational**: Task demands, Role demands, Role conflict, Role overload, Role ambiguity, Interpersonal demands (group dynamics, demanding boss, unpleasant coworkers), Distractions, Admin policies.
  3. **Personal**: Family/personal relationships, Economic problems.
* **Intervening Variables**: Education and health, nature of job, self-efficacy, psychological disposition (rumination).
* **Consequences of Stress**: Physiological, Psychological, Behavioral, Organizational.
* **Preventive Stress Management**:
  * **Individual**: Time management, physical management (exercise, diet), psychological management (mindfulness, relaxation), social management (support), self-awareness.
  * **Organizational**: Goal Setting (MBO), Job Enrichment/Design, Role Clarity, Open Communication, Counselling/EAP (Employee Assistance Programs), reducing workload.

## 7. Motivation Concepts

* **Definition**: The processes that account for an individual’s **intensity** (how hard they try), **direction** (consistent with goals), and **persistence** (how long they maintain effort) toward attaining an organizational goal.

### A. Early Theories
1. **Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs**: Five needs (Physiological -> Safety -> Social -> Esteem -> Self-Actualization). Individuals cannot move to the next higher level until current level is satisfied.
2. **McGregor’s Theory X and Y**:
   * *Theory X* (Negative): Workers have little ambition, dislike work, avoid responsibility.
   * *Theory Y* (Positive): Workers are self-directed, enjoy work, accept responsibility.
3. **Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory**: Satisfaction and dissatisfaction are separate constructs.
   * *Hygiene Factors* (Extrinsic): Prevent dissatisfaction (Company policies, Salary, Work conditions).
   * *Motivators* (Intrinsic): Cause satisfaction (Growth, Responsibility, Achievement).
4. **McClelland’s Three Needs Theory**:
   * *nAch* (Need for Achievement): Drive to excel and succeed.
   * *nPow* (Need for Power): Need to make others behave in a certain way.
   * *nAff* (Need for Affiliation): Desire for friendly/close relationships.

### B. Contemporary Theories
1. **Locke’s Goal-Setting Theory**: **Specific** and **difficult** goals, with self-generated feedback, lead to higher performance. Difficult goals direct attention, energize, increase persistence, and force efficiency.
2. **Bandura’s Self-Efficacy Theory**: Belief that one is capable of performing a task. Complements Goal-Setting. Increased by:
   * *Enactive mastery*: Relevant experience ("practice makes perfect").
   * *Vicarious modeling*: Watching others (especially similar others) do it.
   * *Verbal persuasion*: Pygmalion/Galatea effect (self-fulfilling prophecies).
   * *Arousal*: Getting "psyched up".
3. **Adams’ Equity Theory**: Employees compare outcomes-to-inputs ratios with "Relevant Others" (Self-Inside, Self-Outside, Other-Inside, Other-Outside).
   * Equal ratios = Equity (fairness).
   * Unequal ratios = Tension (Underrewarded -> Anger; Overrewarded -> Guilt). Tension motivates action to restore equity.
4. **Vroom’s Expectancy Theory**: The strength of a tendency to act depends on:
   * **Expectancy**: Effort -> Performance relationship.
   * **Instrumentality**: Performance -> Reward relationship.
   * **Valence**: Reward -> Personal Goals relationship (valuation of the reward in the employee's eyes).

## 8. The Johari Window (Self-Awareness Model)
* **ARENA (Known to self, Known to others)**: "You know. They know." Expand this by sharing strengths, working out loud, and building connections.
* **BLIND SPOT (Not known to self, Known to others)**: "They know, You don't." Shrink this by asking others for feedback.
* **FAÇADE / HIDDEN AREA (Known to self, Not known to others)**: "You know, They don't." Minimize this by opening up about struggles/aspirations.
* **UNKNOWN (Not known to self, Not known to others)**: "You don't know, They don't know." The space for self-discovery and hidden talents.

## 9. Integration: Applying Theories to Case Studies & PYQs

* **Torfs Shoe Retailer**: A perfect example of shifting from Classical Bureaucracy to the Human Relations approach. Empowering employees to design a sneaker provided **Motivators** (Herzberg), fulfilled **nAff** and **nAch** (McClelland), and eliminated alienation, leading to exceptionally low turnover.
* **Round-the-Clock Stress (ABC Engineering)**: 24/7 demands (Technological/Economic environmental stressors) require employees with high **Emotional Stability**, **Internal Locus of Control**, and **Type B** traits to avoid severe psychological consequences. 
* **Thanks for Nothing (Verbal Praise)**: Verbal praise fails when managers lack **Emotional Intelligence** (empathy) or give it arbitrarily. To work, praise must be linked to specific performance (**Expectancy Theory**) and paired with tangible rewards to prevent it from becoming an empty, patronizing gesture.
* **Call Centre Hiring**: Recruiters should look for **High Emotional Stability** (to handle stress), **High Agreeableness** (for warmth), and strong **EI**. Allowing abusive customers purely for profit is an unethical return to **Scientific Management**, treating employees as mere instruments and violating the dignity highlighted by the **Human Relations approach**.
* **Virtual Teams (HubSpot Remote Week)**: Using the **Johari Window** to expand the "Arena" (openness) via Slack and video messages builds trust and overcomes the conformity/isolation pressures (Asch's experiment) that make remote workers hesitate to speak up.
