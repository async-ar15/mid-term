## 1. Quiz 1: Torfs Case Study & Motivation Theories

**Q1. Maslow vs. Herzberg: Which view do you agree with and why? (7.5 marks)**
* **Solution:** I agree more with Herzberg’s view that the first three needs (Physiological, Safety, Social) act primarily as **Hygiene factors** in a modern workplace. According to Herzberg, factors like salary, job security, and working conditions (which map to Maslow's lower-order needs) only prevent dissatisfaction; their presence does not actively drive employees to perform better. True motivation comes from intrinsic **Motivators** such as achievement, recognition, responsibility, and growth (which map to Maslow's higher-order needs). However, it is important to note that Maslow's view holds true in extreme situations—if an employee is struggling with basic survival or safety (e.g., severe economic problems), those lower-order needs will absolutely act as primary motivators until they are satisfied.

**Q2. Designing a reward system using Expectancy Theory (7.5 marks)**
* **Solution:** As a manager, I would design a reward system by strengthening every link in the Expectancy Theory chain:
  * **Effort -> Performance (Expectancy):** I would provide extensive instruction, training, and resources so employees confidently believe that putting in effort will actually result in high performance. 
  * **Performance -> Reward (Instrumentality):** I would establish clear, written rules and performance standards. Employees must know exactly what level of performance guarantees a specific reward, eliminating any ambiguity or perceived favoritism.
  * **Reward -> Personal Goal (Valence):** Since different employees are motivated by different things, I would offer a flexible menu of rewards (e.g., financial bonuses, extra time off, public recognition, or promotion opportunities). This ensures the reward aligns with what the individual employee actually values and desires.

**Q3(a). Torfs' shift from classical to people-centered management & Employee Commitment (8 marks)**
* **Solution:** Torfs represents a clear shift away from **Classical Management** (which views the worker merely as an instrument of production who executes top-down orders with minimum manager-worker interaction). Instead, Torfs adopted the **Human Relations approach**, treating workers as thinking, social individuals. By asking employees to help define company values and form small groups to design a sneaker, management engaged in active consultation and participation. 
* **Organizational Commitment:** The employees display high commitment because they are given autonomy and responsibility, effectively preventing **alienation** from the work process. The exceptionally low employee turnover reflects this strong commitment; employees are emotionally attached, feel their human dignity is respected, and experience a "family feel" that keeps them loyal to the organization.

**Q3(b). Motivation theories supporting these methods (7 marks)**
* **Solution:** 
  * **Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory:** Torfs focuses heavily on **Motivators**. Giving employees the freedom to define values and design products provides them with *responsibility, achievement, and growth*, which leads to high job satisfaction.
  * **McClelland's Three-Needs Theory:** Placing employees in small groups to collaboratively design a sneaker perfectly satisfies the **Need for Affiliation**. Giving them a voice in company direction satisfies the **Need for Power**, and producing a winning design satisfies the **Need for Achievement**.
  * **Maslow's Hierarchy:** The company’s philanthropic efforts (e.g., building orphanages) and empowering environment help employees reach **Self-actualization** and **Esteem**, going far beyond basic physiological and safety needs.

## 2. Case Study: Round-the-Clock Stress

**(a) Personality types for psychological resilience & reaction to sudden 24/7 on-call announcement**
* **Resilient Personality Types:** 
  * **Emotional Stability:** Essential for managing the anxiety and pressure of non-standard hours and fatigue.
  * **Internal Locus of Control:** Individuals who believe "I control what happens to me" are better at actively managing their schedules and adapting, rather than feeling victimized.
  * **High Self-monitoring:** The ability to adjust behavior to varying external situations (like switching between day and night shifts).
  * **Type B Personality:** Being less time-urgent and capable of relaxing without guilt helps mitigate the continuous stress of a 24/7 environment.
* **Reaction to Sudden Announcement:** Employees with these resilient traits would likely adapt constructively, finding practical ways to reorganize their lives. Conversely, employees lacking these traits (e.g., Type A individuals obsessed with structured achievement, or those with an external locus of control) would likely experience severe frustration, burnout, and role conflict.

**(b) Environmental sources of stress indicated in the case**
* **Technological Uncertainty/Change:** Advanced technology has eroded the boundary between work and home, enabling and demanding 24-hour connectivity.
* **Economic and Competitive Pressures:** Businesses are forced to operate 24/7 to boost profits (e.g., the 5% profit increase) and survive in an environment of intense competition and shrinking margins.
* **Globalization:** The need for international businesses, like brokerage firms, to match global market timings (Europe and Asia) creates environmental demands that directly stress employees.

## 3. Case Incident 2: Thanks for Nothing

**Q1. Why don't companies and managers often use verbal praise?**
* **Solution:** Managers may lack Emotional Intelligence (specifically empathy and social skills). Bureaucratic structures often breed **impersonality**, where officials perform duties without giving personal consideration to individuals. Furthermore, as the case shows, praise handed out arbitrarily ("U Done Good" badges) without genuine thought feels patronizing and insincere.

**Q2. Steps to motivate employees after observing them perform well**
* **Solution:** Applying **Expectancy Theory**, I would ensure the praise is explicitly linked to the specific accomplishment (strengthening the performance-reward link). Applying **Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory**, I would ensure that verbal praise is paired with tangible **Motivators** (such as new responsibilities, opportunities for growth, or a raise) to ensure the reward has high valence for the employee.

**Q3. Downsides to too much verbal praise and how to alleviate them**
* **Solution:** Overuse of verbal praise dilutes its value, making it an empty gesture. It breaks the Expectancy Theory link if everyone gets praised regardless of actual effort. To alleviate this, praise should be reserved for specific, exceptional achievements, and managers must ensure it is paired periodically with tangible benefits (money, promotion).

**Q4. Ensuring recognition is distributed fairly and justly**
* **Solution:** Implement clear, **written rules** (a characteristic of bureaucracy) that establish specific performance standards required for recognition. This removes favoritism and ensures objective evaluation.

## 4. PYQ: Mid-Semester Examination 2025 (4.jpeg)

**1(a). What is meant by Emotional Intelligence? (4 marks)**
* **Solution:** Emotional Intelligence (EI) is the ability to understand and manage one's own emotions and deal effectively with others. It comprises five components (SAMSE): **Self-awareness** (knowing how you feel), **Managing emotions** (controlling anxiety/anger), **Motivating oneself** (optimism despite barriers), **Empathy** (understanding others' feelings), and **Social skills** (getting along with others).

**1(b). EI vs. Impression Management / Role Enactment (6 marks)**
* **Solution:** EI is distinct from scripted role enactment because EI requires genuine internal **self-awareness** and **managing emotions**. Impression management (like the resort's "Guest Experience Excellence Program") simply forces employees to display outward warmth (surface acting) without addressing their internal feelings. Scripted empathy is **not** true Emotional Intelligence because it lacks authentic empathy and internal emotional regulation. This disconnect is exactly why the resort staff is experiencing emotional exhaustion and burnout.

## 5. PYQ: Mid-Semester Examination 2026 (WhatsApp Image)

**1. Limitations of bureaucratic structures in contemporary settings (7.5 marks)**
* **Solution:** While bureaucracies provide order, they have severe limitations today:
  * **Division of Labor:** Leads to advanced job fragmentation, resulting in **alienation** (workers lose connection to the product) and **trained incapacity** (developing blind spots).
  * **Written Rules:** Can lead to **goal displacement**, where employees care more about following the rules than achieving the actual organizational goal.
  * **Impersonality:** Leads to frustration and disaffection among employees and customers.
  * **Inefficiency:** As explained by **Parkinson's Law** (work expands to fill time, multiplying subordinates needlessly) and the **Peter Principle** (employees rise to their level of incompetence). Contemporary organizations require flexibility and speed, which rigid bureaucracies prevent.

**2. Strongest motivation theory in contemporary settings (7.5 marks)**
* **Solution:** **Vroom's Expectancy Theory** offers the strongest practical value. Contemporary workforces are highly diverse, meaning different people want different things. Expectancy theory accounts for this through **Valence** (the personal value of a reward). It practically guides managers to ensure employees have the tools to succeed (Effort -> Performance), sets clear metrics (Performance -> Reward), and offers flexible benefits to suit individual needs, unlike Maslow or Herzberg which assume universal needs.

**3. Id, Ego, and Superego (Freud) (7.5 marks)**
* **Solution:** 
  * **Id:** Present from birth, unconscious, and driven by the **pleasure** principle ("I want it").
  * **Ego:** Driven by the **reality** principle, balancing the Id.
  * **Superego:** Driven by **morality** and ethical values ("Is it right?").
  * **Early Childhood:** Experiences shape their balance. For example, a child raised with excessively strict rules and punishment may develop an overbearing Superego, leading to intense guilt and anxiety. A child given everything they want without boundaries may develop a dominant Id, leading to impulsive, narcissistic, or selfish behavior in adulthood. A healthy environment helps the Ego become strong enough to practically balance the two.

**4. Recruiter for a Call Centre (7.5 marks)**
* **Personality Types to Hire:** 
  * **High Emotional Stability:** To remain calm and manage the stress of abusive calls.
  * **High Agreeableness:** To remain cooperative, warm, and courteous.
  * **Type B:** Less impatient and more capable of handling tedious calls without frustration.
* **Role of EI:** High EI is crucial. **Self-awareness** helps the employee recognize their rising anger, **Managing emotions** prevents them from lashing out at the customer, and **Empathy** helps them de-escalate the situation by understanding the customer's frustration.
* **Ethical Implications:** Allowing abuse for profit violates the **Human Relations** approach. It treats the worker merely as an instrument of production (**Scientific Management** view), ignoring their human dignity. Ethically, organizations have a responsibility to manage occupational stress and protect employee well-being; prioritizing profit over mental health leads to severe psychological consequences and alienation.
