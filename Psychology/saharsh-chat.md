🔥 So how did Hawthorne challenge Taylor?

Memorize these 4 points:

① Taylor → economic/technical factors

Taylor emphasized:

efficiency + task design + time + incentives.

Hawthorne → social factors

Workers are influenced by:

groups + relationships + supervision + social norms.

② Taylor → worker as individual

Scientific Management largely treats the worker as an isolated production unit.

Hawthorne → worker as social being

Workers are influenced by their co-workers and informal groups.

③ Taylor → management controls work

Management designs the work and workers execute it.

Hawthorne → informal groups have power

Workers can develop their own norms that influence productivity.

④ Taylor → productivity through efficiency/incentives

Hawthorne showed that attention, relationships, motivation and group dynamics can influence productivity.

📝 Perfect 5-mark answer

If this comes tomorrow, write something like:

The Hawthorne experiments challenged Scientific Management by showing that productivity is influenced not only by technical and economic factors but also by social and psychological factors. Taylor's Scientific Management emphasized scientific analysis of tasks, division of labour, time studies and financial incentives to maximize efficiency.

However, the Hawthorne experiments showed the importance of human relationships and group behaviour. In the illumination experiment, productivity increased in both groups even when lighting was reduced. The Bank Wiring Observation Room further showed that workers developed group norms and social pressures that influenced their level of output.

Thus, while Scientific Management viewed efficiency and task design as central to productivity, the Hawthorne findings emphasized that workers are social individuals whose behaviour is influenced by informal groups, relationships and managerial attention.

PART 3 — PARKINSON'S LAW

Proposed by Cyril Northcote Parkinson in 1955.

Main principle

Work expands so as to fill the time available for its completion.

Meaning: if someone has a lot of time to complete a task, the task may consume all of that available time even if it could have been completed much sooner. 
Lecture 2

Bureaucratic implications

Parkinson identified:

multiplication of subordinates
bureaucrats creating work for other bureaucrats
unnecessary expansion of hierarchy

A bureaucratic pyramid can therefore grow even when the actual amount of work does not increase. 
Lecture 2

Diminishing returns

Extra time/effort does not necessarily produce proportionally better results.

Therefore, productivity requires finding the point where additional effort stops producing sufficient additional benefit. 
Lecture 2

Exam keyword:
Parkinson's Law = criticism of bureaucratic inefficiency.
