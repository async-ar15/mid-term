# 4. Diffusion Models

Welcome to the final module! Diffusion Models are currently the state-of-the-art in generative AI (powering models like DALL-E 2, Midjourney, and Stable Diffusion). They work by slowly destroying data with noise, and then learning to reverse that exact process to generate new data from pure noise.

---

## 1. Training & Sampling Pipeline

The diffusion model pipeline consists of two separate processes:
1. **The Forward Process (Training):** Also known as the "noising" process. We take a real image from our dataset and systematically add tiny amounts of Gaussian noise to it over $T$ steps until the image is completely destroyed and becomes pure random noise. This process is fixed (no learned parameters).
2. **The Reverse Process (Sampling):** Also known as the "denoising" process. We start with pure Gaussian noise and train a neural network (usually a U-Net) to sequentially remove the noise, step-by-step, until a crisp, realistic image emerges.

---

## 2. Mathematical Formulation

### 2.1 The Forward Process (Markov Chain)
The forward process is a Markov chain, meaning each step $x_t$ only depends on the immediately preceding step $x_{t-1}$. 
The transition probability is defined as:
$$q(x_t | x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{1 - \beta_t}x_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I})$$

* **What is $\beta_t$?**
  $\beta_t \in (0, 1)$ is the **variance schedule** (or noise schedule). It defines exactly how much noise is added at step $t$. Typically, $\beta_t$ is kept very small and gradually increases as $t \to T$. 
* **Significance of $\beta_t$:** It controls the destruction of information. By keeping $\beta_t$ small, each step makes only a tiny change, allowing the reverse process to be modeled as a Gaussian. 

### 2.2 Convergence to Pure Noise ($x_T$)
Using the reparameterization trick repeatedly, we can sample $x_t$ directly from the original image $x_0$ without simulating all intermediate steps.
Let $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$. The closed-form equation is:
$$q(x_t | x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I})$$

**Why $x_T$ converges to Gaussian noise:** 
As $t \to T$, the product of many fractions ($\alpha_s < 1$) causes $\bar{\alpha}_T$ to approach $0$. 
Substituting $\bar{\alpha}_T \approx 0$ into the equation yields $\mathcal{N}(x_T; 0, \mathbf{I})$. Thus, all information from $x_0$ is destroyed, leaving pure standard Gaussian noise.

### 2.3 The Generative (Reverse) Process
The reverse process asks: given a noisy image $x_t$, what was the slightly less noisy image $x_{t-1}$? We model this using a neural network with parameters $\theta$:
$$p_\theta(x_{t-1} | x_t) = \mathcal{N}(x_{t-1}; \mu_\theta(x_t, t), \Sigma_\theta(x_t, t))$$

**Relation to VAEs / Variational Inference:**
Just like VAEs, the true reverse distribution $q(x_{t-1} | x_t)$ is intractable. Diffusion models are essentially hierarchical VAEs with $T$ latent layers! We train the network $p_\theta$ to approximate $q$ by **maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)** (or minimizing the variational upper bound on negative log-likelihood). The loss function eventually simplifies to predicting the added noise $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$ using a simple Mean Squared Error (MSE).

### 2.4 Stochastic Differential Equations (SDE)
When we take the limit as the number of time steps $T \to \infty$, the discrete Markov chain becomes a continuous process described by SDEs.
* **Forward SDE:** Describes how data flows into noise over continuous time $t \in [0, 1]$.
  $$dx = f(x, t)dt + g(t)dw$$
  *(where $w$ is standard Brownian motion/Wiener process)*
* **Reverse SDE:** Describes how noise flows back into data.
  $$dx = [f(x, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)]dt + g(t)d\bar{w}$$
  *(where $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ is the "score function", which the neural network learns to estimate).*

## 3. Challenges & Solutions

While diffusion models generate incredibly high-quality images, they have two major flaws:

### 1. Slow Sampling
* **The Problem:** Because the reverse process is a step-by-step Markov chain, generating a single image might require passing it through the massive U-Net 1,000 times!
* **Solutions:** 
  * **DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models):** Bypasses the strict Markov property, allowing the model to safely skip steps during inference (e.g., generating an image in 50 steps instead of 1,000) without retraining.
  * **ODE Solvers / SDE Solvers:** Using advanced numerical solvers to integrate the reverse process faster.
  * **Progressive Distillation:** Training a smaller "student" model to take larger steps by learning from the slow "teacher" model.

### 2. High-Resolution & Conditional Generation
* **The Problem:** Running a U-Net on a $1024 \times 1024$ image for 1,000 steps is computationally impossible on most GPUs. Also, how do we force the model to generate a *specific* image (like a prompt)?
* **Solutions:**
  * **Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs / Stable Diffusion):** Instead of diffusing the massive pixel space, we first use a VAE encoder to compress the image into a tiny latent space. We run the entire diffusion process in this small latent space, and then use the VAE decoder to upscale it back to pixels. This drastically reduces computation.
  * **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** To conditionally guide the generation (e.g., text-to-image), we train the model to understand prompts. During sampling, we extrapolate the noise prediction in the direction of the text prompt, pushing the image to strongly match the user's text.

## 4. Exam Practice Questions & Solutions

### Q1. Diffusion Pipeline & Math (PYQ)
**Question:** Explain the complete training and sampling pipeline. Given $q(x_t | x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{1 - \beta_t}x_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I})$, answer: (A) What is $\beta_t$? (B) Formulate $x_t$ as a Markov process and explain convergence to noise. (C) Write generative process $p_\theta$ and its relation to VAE. (D) Represent Forward/Reverse in SDE.

**Solution:**
* **Pipeline Summary:** 
  * **Training:** We take $x_0$, pick a random timestep $t$, apply noise to get $x_t$, and train a U-Net to predict the exact noise $\epsilon$ that was added.
  * **Sampling:** We start with $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and iteratively use the trained U-Net to predict and subtract noise, moving backward from $t=T$ down to $t=0$ to get a clean image.
* **(A) $\beta_t$:** It is the variance schedule, controlling the amount of noise added at step $t$. It is crucial because keeping $\beta_t$ small ensures the reverse process $p(x_{t-1}|x_t)$ is approximately Gaussian, making it possible for a neural network to learn it.
* **(B) Markov Process & Convergence:** The process is Markovian because $x_t$ depends only on $x_{t-1}$. Using the reparameterization trick, we can jump straight to $t$: $x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon$. Because $\alpha_t < 1$, as $t \to T$, $\bar{\alpha}_T \to 0$. The mean term $\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0$ disappears, and the variance term becomes $1 \cdot \epsilon$, resulting in pure standard normal noise $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
* **(C) Generative Process & VAE relation:** $p_\theta(x_{t-1} | x_t) = \mathcal{N}(x_{t-1}; \mu_\theta(x_t, t), \Sigma_\theta(x_t, t))$. Like VAEs, diffusion models maximize the ELBO. A diffusion model can be viewed as a hierarchical VAE with a fixed, parameter-free encoder (the forward noise process) and a highly expressive decoder (the reverse denoising U-Net) spread across $T$ layers.
* **(D) SDE Representation:** 
  * Forward: $dx = f(x, t)dt + g(t)dw$
  * Reverse: $dx = [f(x, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)]dt + g(t)d\bar{w}$

### Q2. Diffusion Challenges (PYQ OR Question)
**Question:** Discuss strategies to accelerate sampling in diffusion models. Explain how diffusion models are adapted for high-resolution conditional generation.

**Solution:**
* **Accelerating Sampling:** The standard DDPM requires hundreds or thousands of steps because the Gaussian assumption only holds for small noise increments. 
  * **Strategy - DDIM:** Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models rewrite the forward process to be non-Markovian while maintaining the same marginal distributions. This deterministic sampling approach allows the model to safely skip steps during inference (e.g., from 1000 steps down to 50) without retraining the network.
* **High-Resolution Conditional Generation:**
  * **High-Resolution Strategy - Latent Diffusion:** Operating on high-res pixel space is extremely memory and compute-heavy. We adapt by using an Autoencoder to map images into a much smaller, compressed latent space. The diffusion process happens entirely in this latent space. A final decoder maps the generated latent back to high-res pixels.
  * **Conditional Generation Strategy - Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** To guide generation based on text, we train the model jointly on conditional (with text) and unconditional (without text) inputs. During sampling, we evaluate both and push the final prediction away from the unconditional prediction and toward the conditional one, resulting in a highly accurate, prompt-aligned image.
