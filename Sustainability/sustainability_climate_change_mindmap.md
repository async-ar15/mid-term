## 🔬 UNIT 1 — The Science of Climate Change (Detailed)

```mermaid
mindmap
  root(("🔬 UNIT 1\nClimate Science"))
    🌡️ Key Distinctions
      Weather
        Short-term atmospheric conditions
        Specific place & time
      Climate
        Long-term pattern of weather
        Statistical averages over 30+ years
      Climate Change
        Broad long-term change in climate system
        IPCC definition — any change over time
        UNFCCC definition — emphasizes human influence
      Global Warming
        Specifically the long-term rise in average global temperature
    🌐 Earth as a System
      Atmosphere
        Gases surrounding Earth
        Contains greenhouse gases
      Hydrosphere
        Oceans, rivers, lakes
        Ocean = huge heat buffer
      Cryosphere
        Ice sheets, glaciers, sea ice, permafrost
        Albedo effect — reflects sunlight
      Lithosphere
        Rocks, soil, sediments
        Stores carbon in geological formations
      Biosphere
        Living organisms
        Forests as carbon sinks
      System Behaviour
        Nodes interact continuously
        Exchange heat, water, carbon
        Feedback loops amplify changes
    ☀️ Energy Balance
      Incoming Solar Radiation
        Provides energy to Earth
      Albedo Effect
        Clouds, ice, bright surfaces reflect sunlight
      Absorption & Re-emission
        Earth absorbs remainder
        Re-emits as infrared radiation
      Natural Greenhouse Effect
        GHGs absorb outgoing IR
        Re-radiate back to surface
        Without it — Earth would be minus 18C
        With it — Earth is roughly plus 15C
      The Problem
        NOT the greenhouse effect itself
        Human AMPLIFICATION of it
    💨 Greenhouse Gases
      CO2 — Carbon Dioxide
        Dominant due to enormous quantities
        Long atmospheric lifetime
        Pre-industrial 280 ppm to now 420+ ppm
      CH4 — Methane
        More potent per molecule than CO2
        Shorter-lived
        Sources — agriculture, wetlands, fossil fuels
      N2O — Nitrous Oxide
        Even more potent than methane
        Agriculture, industrial processes
      SF6 — Sulfur Hexafluoride
        Extremely high GWP
        Industrial applications
      GWP — Global Warming Potential
        Converts all gases to CO2-equivalent
        Common unit for comparison
        Depends on time horizon
    📈 Evidence of Warming
      Thermometer Records
        Direct temperature measurements
      Ice Cores
        Extend record 800,000 years
        Trapped air bubbles show past CO2
      Keeling Curve
        Continuous CO2 at Mauna Loa since 1958
        Shows relentless long-term increase
      Satellites
        Global temperature monitoring
      Glacier Observations
        Worldwide retreat documented
      Sea-Level Measurements
        Thermal expansion plus ice melt
      Proxy Data
        Tree rings and corals
        Independent confirmation
    🔄 Carbon Cycle
      Fast Carbon Cycle
        Days to centuries
        Atmosphere to biosphere to ocean surface
        Photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition
      Slow Geological Cycle
        Thousands to millions of years
        Rock weathering, sedimentation
        Fossil fuels locked away
      Human Disruption
        Took carbon from slow cycle
        Rapidly injected into fast cycle
        Fossil fuel combustion
        Deforestation — releases stored carbon AND destroys sinks
      Planetary Accounting
        Natural — sources approx equal sinks
        Human — additional sources exceed sink capacity
        Result — atmospheric CO2 rises
    🔁 Feedback Loops
      Ice-Albedo Feedback — POSITIVE
        Warming then ice melts
        Less sunlight reflected
        More solar energy absorbed
        More warming
      Permafrost Feedback — POSITIVE
        Warming then permafrost thaws
        CH4 and CO2 released
        More warming then more thawing
      Water Vapour Feedback — POSITIVE
        Warming then more evaporation
        Water vapour is a GHG
        More warming
      Why It Matters
        Makes climate change NONLINEAR
        Small changes can trigger cascading effects
        Connects to CS — modelling and AI
    🏭 Human Causes
      Fossil Fuel Combustion
        Coal, oil, natural gas
        Energy, transport, industry
      Deforestation
        Releases stored carbon
        Destroys future carbon sinks
      Industrial Activity
        Cement, steel, chemicals
      Agriculture
        Methane from livestock & rice
        N2O from fertilizers
      Land-Use Change
        Converting forests, wetlands
      Key Fact
        Dominant driver since circa 1950
        Natural factors insufficient to explain observed warming
```

## 🧪 UNIT 2 — Four Practical Tests of Sustainability (Detailed)

```mermaid
mindmap
  root(("🧪 Four Tests of\nSustainability"))
    🌿 1 — Ecological Integrity
      Definition
        Ecosystems remain functional & resilient
      Components
        Composition — species diversity
        Structure — physical organization
        Function — nutrient cycling, energy flow
        Resilience — ability to recover from disturbance
      Planetary Boundaries Framework
        Scientific limits for human activity
        Nine planetary boundaries identified
        Six transgressed — Richardson et al 2023
        Safe operating space for humanity
    ⚡ 2 — Resource Efficiency
      Definition
        More value per unit of resource
      Decoupling
        Economic growth FROM resource consumption
        Relative decoupling — intensity falls but total rises
        Absolute decoupling — total resource use falls
      IPAT Equation
        I = P x A x T
        I = Environmental Impact
        P = Population
        A = Affluence per capita
        T = Technology — environmental intensity
      IPAT Example
        Pop plus10 pct x Affluence plus20 pct x Tech minus25 pct
        1.10 x 1.20 x 0.75 = 0.99
        Impact roughly unchanged
      Jevons Paradox — CRITICAL WARNING
        Efficiency reduces cost per unit
        Cheaper resource then MORE total use
        Efficiency alone does not equal sustainability
        Need — hard limits, pricing, behavioural change
    👥 3 — Social Equity
      Justice Dimension
        Benefits & burdens distributed fairly
        Not sustainable if pollution falls on powerless communities
      Distributive Justice
        Fair allocation of resources & impacts
      Procedural Justice
        Fair processes in decision-making
        Affected communities have voice
    💰 4 — Economic Viability
      Definition
        Activity continues after real costs included
      Externalities
        Costs imposed on society not in market price
        Example — 100 rupee product plus 30 rupee pollution = 130 true cost
      True Social Cost
        Market Price plus Externalized Costs
        Argument for environmental taxation
        Argument for carbon pricing
```

## 📦 UNIT 2 — Carbon Accounting (Detailed)

```mermaid
mindmap
  root(("📦 Carbon\nAccounting"))
    📏 Core Concept
      Cannot manage what you cannot measure
      Convert all GHGs to common unit
      tCO2e — tonnes of CO2 equivalent
    🔢 Calculation Formula
      Emissions = Activity Data x Emission Factor x GWP
      Activity Data — quantity of activity
      Emission Factor — emissions per unit activity
      GWP — Global Warming Potential conversion
    🎯 Three Scopes
      Scope 1 — DIRECT Emissions
        Sources owned or controlled
        Generators & boilers
        Company vehicles
        Fugitive refrigerants
        Process emissions
      Scope 2 — PURCHASED ENERGY
        Indirect from purchased electricity
        Purchased steam
        Purchased heating or cooling
      Scope 3 — VALUE CHAIN
        Everything else upstream & downstream
        Purchased materials & services
        Transportation & distribution
        Business travel
        Employee commuting
        Product use phase
        End-of-life treatment
        Investments
    📊 GreenTech Case Study
      Scope 1 — 120 tCO2e
      Scope 2 — 1454 tCO2e
      Scope 3 — approx 8500 tCO2e
      Total — 10074 tCO2e
      Scope 3 = 84 percent of total footprint
    💡 Crucial Insight
      Scope 3 is often the LARGEST
      Focus only on Scope 1 & 2 = miss most impact
      This is why you MUST go into the supply chain
      Direct connection to Unit 3
```

## 💹 UNIT 2 — Carbon Pricing & Markets (Detailed)

```mermaid
mindmap
  root(("💹 Carbon Pricing\n& Markets"))
    🧠 Economic Foundation
      Externality — pollution cost not in market price
      Goal — make emitting have a financial cost
      Internalize the externality
    💲 Carbon Tax
      Government FIXES the price of carbon
      Resulting emissions quantity is UNCERTAIN
      Simple to implement
      Revenue can fund green transition
    📉 Cap-and-Trade
      Government FIXES total quantity — the cap
      Market determines the price
      Process
        Set cap on total emissions
        Distribute allowances
        Companies trade allowances
        Surrender allowances for actual emissions
      Allowance = legal permission to emit 1 tCO2e
    🎫 Allowance vs Credit — CRITICAL DISTINCTION
      Allowance
        Permission to emit
        Part of compliance system
        Created by government
      Carbon Credit or Offset
        Claimed reduction or removal ELSEWHERE
        Voluntary or compliance markets
        Represents emission avoided or removed
    ✅ Four Credibility Tests for Credits
      Additionality
        Reduction would NOT have happened without credit incentive
      Permanence
        Reduction remains valid over time
        Forest could burn down
      Verification
        Independent third-party confirmation
      No Double Counting
        Same reduction NOT claimed multiple times
    ⚠️ Verra Case
      Credits can exist in registry
      Can be verified under methodology
      BUT questions remain about genuine additionality
      Accurate quantification challenged
    🇮🇳 India CCTS
      Carbon Credit Trading Scheme
      NOT absolute emissions cap
      Uses emissions-INTENSITY targets
        Emissions per unit of output
      Better than target then earn Carbon Credit Certificates
      Worse than target then must buy certificates
      Rationale
        Allow industrial production to continue
        Make each unit progressively cleaner
    🔗 Carbon Markets Logic Chain
      Measurement then Target then Financial Incentive then Change then Verification
      Technology role — MRV, satellites, blockchain, AI, registries
```

```mermaid
mindmap
```

```mermaid
    🏭 Production Tools
      Energy efficiency in manufacturing
      Water efficiency & recycling
      Waste minimization
      Safer chemical substitution
      Process optimization
      Material substitution
    🔄 Life Cycle Assessment — LCA
      Purpose
        Prevent looking at only ONE stage
        Holistic environmental impact view
      Three Boundaries
        Cradle-to-Gate
          Raw material to factory gate
          Excludes use & disposal
        Cradle-to-Grave
          Extraction to production to use to disposal
          Full lifecycle but linear
        Cradle-to-Cradle
          Materials become inputs for another cycle
          Connects to CIRCULAR ECONOMY
    📐 Material Substitution Example
      Virgin Plastic
        0.050 kg x 3.5 kgCO2e per kg = 0.175 kgCO2e per unit
      Recycled Plastic
        0.050 kg x 1.2 kgCO2e per kg = 0.060 kgCO2e per unit
      Saving per unit
        0.115 kgCO2e
      At 1 million units per year
        115,000 kg = 115 tonnes CO2e saved
    💡 Key Lesson
      Does not always require full product redesign
      Changing material input alone can substantially reduce embodied emissions
```

## ♻️ UNIT 3 — Circular Economy (Detailed)

```mermaid
mindmap
  root(("♻️ Circular\nEconomy"))
    🔀 Linear vs Circular
      Linear — Traditional
        Take then Make then Use then Dispose
        Extraction-based
        Waste-generating
      Circular — New Model
        Design then Use then Reuse then Repair then Remanufacture then Recycle
        Keep materials circulating
        Preserve value
    🏛️ Ellen MacArthur Framework
      Three Core Principles
        1 — Design out waste & pollution
          Eliminate waste at design stage
          Not manage it after creation
        2 — Keep products & materials in use
          Extend product life
          Share, repair, remanufacture
        3 — Regenerate natural systems
          Return biological nutrients safely
          Enhance rather than degrade ecosystems
    🔄 Two Major Loops
      Technical Cycle
        Metals, plastics, electronics, glass
        Remain in industrial circulation
        Maintain, reuse, remanufacture, recycle
      Biological Cycle
        Food, wood, natural fibres, organic materials
        Return safely to natural systems
        Composting, anaerobic digestion
        Regenerate soil & ecosystems
    💡 Critical Insight
      Circular economy is NOT merely recycling
      Recycling is only ONE part
      Objective — preserve value & prevent extraction
      System redesign, not just waste management
```
