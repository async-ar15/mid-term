# Chapter 6: Supply, Demand, and Government Policies (Study Guide)

## Concept 1: Price Ceilings
A price ceiling is a legal maximum on the price at which a good can be sold.
- **Not Binding:** If the price ceiling is set ABOVE the equilibrium price, it has no effect on the market. The market will naturally reach equilibrium.
- **Binding:** If the price ceiling is set BELOW the equilibrium price, it is a binding constraint. Sellers cannot charge the equilibrium price, so they must charge the ceiling price.
- **Result of a Binding Ceiling:** It causes a SHORTAGE (Quantity Demanded > Quantity Supplied). Sellers must ration the scarce goods among buyers (e.g., long lines, seller bias). Rent control is a classic example.

## Concept 2: Price Floors
A price floor is a legal minimum on the price at which a good can be sold.
- **Not Binding:** If the price floor is set BELOW the equilibrium price, it has no effect. The market can still reach equilibrium because the equilibrium price is legally allowed.
- **Binding:** If the price floor is set ABOVE the equilibrium price, it is binding. Prices cannot legally fall to the equilibrium level.
- **Result of a Binding Floor:** It causes a SURPLUS (Quantity Supplied > Quantity Demanded). The minimum wage is a classic example, causing a surplus of labor, which we call unemployment.

## Concept 3: Taxes and Tax Incidence
Taxes drive a wedge between the price buyers pay and the price sellers receive.
- **Effects of a Tax:** It reduces the quantity bought and sold. The effect on the market (prices and quantity) is exactly the same whether the government imposes the tax legally on the buyer or the seller.
- **Tax Incidence:** This describes who actually bears the burden of the tax. The burden depends entirely on the price elasticities of supply and demand, NOT on whom the tax is levied.
- **The Rule of Tax Incidence:** The burden of a tax falls more heavily on the side of the market that is LESS elastic. If demand is inelastic, buyers cannot easily leave the market, so they bear the burden. If supply is inelastic, sellers cannot easily leave the market, so they bear the burden.

## Solved Question: Active Learning 1 (Price Controls)
> **Question:** The market for hotel rooms is in equilibrium. The demand curve goes from P=130, Q=0 to P=40, Q=130. The supply curve goes from P=50, Q=0 to P=130, Q=130. The equilibrium is at P=100, Q=100. Determine the effects of:
> A. 90 dollar price ceiling
> B. 90 dollar price floor
> C. 120 dollar price floor

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
- **A. 90 dollar price ceiling:** 
  - A ceiling is a maximum price. Since the equilibrium is 100, a maximum of 90 prevents the market from reaching equilibrium. It is **binding**.
  - At P = 90, Buyers demand 120 rooms.
  - At P = 90, Sellers supply 90 rooms.
  - *Result:* There is a **shortage** of 30 rooms (120 demanded - 90 supplied).

- **B. 90 dollar price floor:** 
  - A floor is a minimum price. Since the equilibrium is 100, a minimum of 90 is below the equilibrium. The market can still legally charge 100.
  - *Result:* It is **not binding**. The market stays at the equilibrium of P=100, Q=100.

- **C. 120 dollar price floor:**
  - Since the equilibrium is 100, a minimum of 120 prevents the market from reaching equilibrium. It is **binding**.
  - At P = 120, Buyers demand 60 rooms.
  - At P = 120, Sellers supply 120 rooms.
  - *Result:* There is a **surplus** of 60 rooms (120 supplied - 60 demanded).

## Solved Question: Active Learning 2 (Effects of a Tax)
> **Question:** The market for hotel rooms is in equilibrium at P=100, Q=100. Suppose the government imposes a tax on buyers of 30 dollars per room. Find the new Quantity, Price buyers pay, Price sellers receive, and the incidence of the tax.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. A 30 dollar tax on buyers shifts the Demand curve down by exactly 30 dollars at every quantity.
2. The new equilibrium quantity where the new demand curve intersects the supply curve is **Q = 80**.
3. At Q = 80, the price the sellers receive (from the supply curve) is **80 dollars**.
4. At Q = 80, the price the buyers pay is the seller's price plus the tax: 80 + 30 = **110 dollars**. (You can also find this by looking at the original demand curve at Q=80).
5. **Incidence of the tax:**
   - The original price was 100. 
   - Buyers now pay 110. Their burden is 110 - 100 = **10 dollars**.
   - Sellers now receive 80. Their burden is 100 - 80 = **20 dollars**.
   - *Conclusion:* Even though the tax was levied on buyers, sellers bear a larger share of the burden (20 vs 10). This means supply is less elastic than demand in this market.
