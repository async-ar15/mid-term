# Economics Midterm Study Plan

This study plan covers all the PDF materials found in your folder. The chapters range from basic economic principles to the intricacies of markets, government policies, externalities, and production costs.

> [!TIP]
> **Study Strategy**
> Economics is cumulative. Ensure you have a solid grasp of supply and demand (Chapters 4-6) before moving to advanced topics like taxation, international trade, or externalities.

## Phase 1: Core Fundamentals

**1. Chapter 1: Ten Principles of Economics**
- **Focus**: Understand the foundational concepts (opportunity cost, marginal thinking, incentives).
- **Goal**: Be able to list and briefly explain each of the 10 principles with real-world examples.

**2. Chapter 3: Interdependence and the Gains from Trade**
- **Focus**: Comparative vs. Absolute Advantage, Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF).
- **Goal**: Be comfortable calculating opportunity costs and determining who should produce which good based on comparative advantage.

## Phase 2: How Markets Work

**3. Chapter 4: The Market Forces of Supply and Demand**
- **Focus**: Shifts vs. movements along the demand/supply curves, market equilibrium, surplus, and shortage.
- **Goal**: Be able to draw and analyze graphs predicting how events affect price and quantity.

**4. Chapter 5: Elasticity and Its Application**
- **Focus**: Price elasticity of demand/supply, cross-price elasticity, income elasticity.
- **Goal**: Memorize the midpoint method formula and understand how elasticity affects total revenue.

**5. Chapter 6: Supply, Demand, and Government Policies**
- **Focus**: Price ceilings, price floors, and the incidence of taxes.
- **Goal**: Understand who bears the burden of a tax and how elasticity plays a role in it.

## Phase 3: Markets and Welfare

**6. Chapter 7: Consumers, Producers, and the Efficiency of Markets**
- **Focus**: Consumer surplus, producer surplus, and total surplus.
- **Goal**: Be able to calculate surplus areas on a graph and understand market efficiency.

**7. Chapter 8: Application: The Costs of Taxation**
- **Focus**: Deadweight loss and how tax size affects it.
- **Goal**: Understand how elasticity determines the size of the deadweight loss and how tax revenue changes with the size of the tax (Laffer curve).

**8. Chapter 9: Application: International Trade**
- **Focus**: World price, winners and losers from trade (importing vs. exporting), tariffs.
- **Goal**: Analyze the welfare effects of a tariff using a supply and demand graph.

## Phase 4: Public Sector and Firm Behavior

**9. Chapter 10: Externalities**
- **Focus**: Negative and positive externalities, Coase theorem, Pigouvian taxes vs. regulations.
- **Goal**: Identify market failures on a graph and explain how internalizing externalities leads to the social optimum.

**10. Chapter 11: Public Goods and Common Resources**
- **Focus**: Excludability and rivalry in consumption, free-rider problem, Tragedy of the Commons.
- **Goal**: Be able to categorize goods (private, public, common resource, club goods) and explain why markets fail to provide public goods efficiently.

**11. Chapter 13: The Cost of Production**
- **Focus**: Fixed vs. variable costs, average vs. marginal costs, economies of scale.
- **Goal**: Understand the shapes of the cost curves and calculate different types of costs from a table.

## Phase 5: Synthesis and Review

**12. Additional Reference Materials**
- **Focus**: Skim through this PDF to see if there are specific case studies, formulas, or summaries provided by the professor.

## PYQ Analysis & Audited Strategy

Based on the previous year's questions, the exam relies heavily on **Calculations**, **Diagrams**, and **Scenario-Based Reasoning**. Here is the pattern and how you should adjust your preparation:

### 1. Question Patterns Observed
- **Calculation Heavy (30-40% of marks):** You must be comfortable with numerical tables. Expect to calculate:
  - Opportunity cost and bounds for gains from trade ($X$ values).
  - Price elasticity using the **Midpoint (arc) Method** and Total Revenue ($TR = P \times Q$).
  - Cost measures (AFC, AVC, ATC, MC) from a given total cost table.
  - Pigovian tax and Deadweight Loss (DWL) areas.
- **Graphical Analysis (30-40% of marks):** Almost every policy or market change requires a "well-labelled diagram". You must practice drawing:
  - Simultaneous shifts in Supply and Demand (and determining the ambiguous effect on P or Q).
  - Tax incidence (showing elastic demand vs. inelastic supply).
  - Externalities (Marginal Private Cost vs. Marginal Social Cost).
- **Conceptual & Application (20-30% of marks):**
  - True/False with explanations (e.g., Absolute vs. Comparative Advantage).
  - Identifying Complements vs. Substitutes and tracing ripple effects across multiple markets.

### 2. Audited Study Plan (High-Yield Focus)
Based on the PYQs, here is where you should spend the majority of your time:

**Tier 1: Guaranteed Calculation & Diagram Topics (Master these first)**
- **Chapter 4 & 5 (Supply, Demand, Elasticity):** This is the core of the midsem. Master the midpoint formula, TR test, and drawing shifting S/D curves for related markets (substitutes/complements).
- **Chapter 3 (Interdependence):** Practice the specific math for comparative advantage. Understand how to find the range of prices/hours where trade is beneficial.
- **Chapter 13 (Cost of Production):** Memorize the formulas for AFC, AVC, ATC, MC and practice filling out cost tables. Know why MC intersects ATC at its minimum.

**Tier 2: Policy and Welfare (Conceptual & Graphical)**
- **Chapter 6 & 8 (Taxes & Welfare):** Focus on tax incidence (who pays the tax based on elasticity) and calculating/drawing Deadweight Loss.
- **Chapter 10 (Externalities):** Practice drawing the divergence between social cost/benefit and private cost/benefit. Know how to calculate the optimal Pigovian tax.
- **Chapter 7 (Efficiency):** Understand the "Invisible Hand" conceptually (as asked in the Endsem PYQ) and how to identify consumer/producer surplus.

**Tier 3: The Rest**
- **Chapter 1 (Ten Principles):** Good for a short 2-3 mark question (like calculating basic opportunity cost).
- **Chapter 9 & 11 (Trade & Public Goods):** Review conceptually, but they appear less prominently in the structured mathematical questions compared to the others.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Actionable Next Step:** Stop reading the PDFs passively. Take a blank sheet of paper and practice drawing the graphs for (1) Tax Incidence, (2) Simultaneous S/D shifts, and (3) Negative Externalities. Then, solve the midpoint elasticity and cost table numericals from the PYQs yourself.
