# Chapter 3: Interdependence and the Gains from Trade (Study Guide)

## Concept 1: Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF)
**Definition:** A graph that shows the combinations of two goods the economy can possibly produce given the available resources and the available technology. Points *on* the line are efficient, points *inside* the line are inefficient, and points *outside* the line are impossible without trade.

## Concept 2: Absolute Advantage
**Definition:** The ability to produce a good using fewer inputs than another producer. 
If Country A can produce a car in 10 hours, and Country B takes 20 hours, Country A has the absolute advantage. 

## Concept 3: Comparative Advantage
**Definition:** The ability to produce a good at a lower **opportunity cost** than another producer. 
This is the core concept of trade. You calculate it by looking at what you have to give up to produce one unit of a good. 
*Formula:* Opportunity cost of Good A = (Amount of Good B given up) / (Amount of Good A gained)

## Concept 4: Who should export/import?
A country should **specialize and export** the good for which it has a comparative advantage (lowest opportunity cost), and **import** the good for which it has a comparative disadvantage.

## Solved Question: PYQ Q2
> **Question:** A German worker takes 400 hours to produce a car and 2 hours to produce a case of wine. A French worker takes 600 hours to produce a car and $X$ hours to produce a case of wine.
> **a.** For what values of $X$ will gains from trade be possible? Explain.
> **b.** For what values of $X$ will Germany export cars and import wine? Explain.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
First, we must find the opportunity cost of 1 car for both countries.
- **Germany:** To make 1 car, they spend 400 hours. In those 400 hours, they *could have* made wine (at 2 hours per case). 
  - Opportunity Cost of 1 car = 400 / 2 = **200 cases of wine**.
- **France:** To make 1 car, they spend 600 hours. In those 600 hours, they *could have* made wine (at $X$ hours per case). 
  - Opportunity Cost of 1 car = **600 / $X$ cases of wine**.

**Part a:**
Gains from trade are possible as long as their opportunity costs are **not equal**. If they are the same, there is no comparative advantage.
- Therefore: 600 / $X$ ≠ 200.
- Solving for $X$: $X$ ≠ 3. 
- *Answer:* Gains from trade are possible for any positive value of $X$ **except $X$ = 3**.

**Part b:**
For Germany to export cars, Germany must have a **comparative advantage in cars**. This means Germany's opportunity cost to make a car must be **strictly less** than France's.
- 200 < 600 / $X$
- Multiply both sides by $X$: 200$X$ < 600
- Divide by 200: $X$ < 3.
- *Answer:* Germany will export cars if **$X$ < 3**.

## Solved Question: PYQ Q3
> **Question:** Are the following statements true or false? Explain in each case.

**a) "Two countries can achieve gains from trade even if one of the countries has an absolute advantage in the production of all goods."**
- **True.** Trade is based on *comparative advantage*, not absolute advantage. As long as their opportunity costs differ, they can specialize and both gain from trade.

**b) "Certain very talented people have a comparative advantage in everything they do."**
- **False.** Comparative advantage compares a person's opportunity cost between two tasks. Because opportunity costs are reciprocals (e.g., if OC of cars is 2 wine, OC of wine is 1/2 car), if you have a lower opportunity cost in one good, you mathematically MUST have a higher opportunity cost in the other good. You cannot have a comparative advantage in everything.

**c) "If a certain trade is good for one person, it can't be good for the other one."**
- **False.** Trade is not a zero-sum game. Because of specialization based on comparative advantage, trade allows both parties to consume outside their own Production Possibilities Frontier. Both win.

**d) "If a certain trade is good for one person, it is always good for the other one."**
- **False.** A specific trade is only mutually beneficial if the "price of the trade" (terms of trade) falls *between* the opportunity costs of the two parties. If a trade is forced at a bad price, one party will lose.

**e) "If trade is good for a country, it must be good for everyone in the country."**
- **False.** Trade increases the total economic pie, making the country as a whole better off. However, trade creates winners and losers within the country (e.g., domestic workers in an industry that now faces cheap foreign imports may lose their jobs).

## Solved Question: Active Learning 1 (Deriving Japan's PPF)
> **Question:** Japan has 30,000 hours of labor per month. Producing one computer requires 125 hours. Producing one ton of wheat requires 25 hours. What are the endpoints of the PPF?

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. If Japan puts ALL labor into computers: 30,000 hours / 125 hours per computer = **240 computers**.
2. If Japan puts ALL labor into wheat: 30,000 hours / 25 hours per ton = **1,200 tons of wheat**.
- *Answer:* The PPF is a straight line from 1,200 on the wheat axis to 240 on the computer axis.

## Solved Question: Active Learning 2 (Production under trade)
> **Question:** The US has 50,000 hours of labor. 1 computer = 100 hours. 1 ton of wheat = 10 hours.
> **A.** If the US produces 3,400 tons of wheat, how many computers can it produce with the remaining labor?
> **B.** If Japan produces 240 computers (from previous question), how much wheat can it produce?

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
**Part A (US):**
1. Labor used for wheat = 3,400 tons * 10 hours = 34,000 hours.
2. Remaining labor = 50,000 total - 34,000 used = 16,000 hours.
3. Computers produced = 16,000 hours / 100 hours per computer = **160 computers**.

**Part B (Japan):**
1. Labor used for computers = 240 computers * 125 hours = 30,000 hours.
2. Remaining labor = 30,000 total - 30,000 used = 0 hours.
3. Wheat produced = **0 tons**.

## Solved Question: Active Learning 3 (Consumption under trade)
> **Question:** The US exports 700 tons of wheat and imports 110 computers from Japan. How much of each good is consumed in the US and Japan? (Use production numbers from Active Learning 2).

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
**US Consumption:**
- Wheat = Produced (3,400) - Exported (700) = **2,700 tons of wheat**.
- Computers = Produced (160) + Imported (110) = **270 computers**.

**Japan Consumption:**
- Wheat = Produced (0) + Imported (700) = **700 tons of wheat**.
- Computers = Produced (240) - Exported (110) = **130 computers**.

## Solved Question: Active Learning 4 (Absolute vs. Comparative Advantage)
> **Question:** Argentina and Brazil both have 10,000 hours of labor.
> Argentina: 1 lb coffee = 2 hours; 1 bottle wine = 4 hours.
> Brazil: 1 lb coffee = 1 hour; 1 bottle wine = 5 hours.
> 1. Which country has the absolute advantage in coffee?
> 2. Which country has the comparative advantage in wine?

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. **Absolute Advantage in Coffee:** Look purely at who is faster. Brazil takes 1 hour, Argentina takes 2 hours. Brazil is faster. 
   - *Answer:* **Brazil** has the absolute advantage in coffee.
2. **Comparative Advantage in Wine:** Find the opportunity cost of 1 bottle of wine for both countries.
   - Argentina: 1 bottle of wine takes 4 hours. In 4 hours, they could have made 2 lbs of coffee (4 hrs / 2 hrs per coffee). OC = **2 lbs coffee**.
   - Brazil: 1 bottle of wine takes 5 hours. In 5 hours, they could have made 5 lbs of coffee (5 hrs / 1 hr per coffee). OC = **5 lbs coffee**.
   - Argentina gives up less coffee (2 < 5) to make wine. 
   - *Answer:* **Argentina** has the comparative advantage in wine.
