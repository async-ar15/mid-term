# Chapter 7: Consumers, Producers, and the Efficiency of Markets (Study Guide)

## Concept 1: Willingness to Pay (WTP) and Consumer Surplus
- **Willingness to Pay (WTP):** The maximum amount a buyer will pay for a good. It represents exactly how much the buyer values the good.
- **Consumer Surplus (CS):** The amount a buyer is willing to pay minus the amount the buyer actually pays. 
  - Formula: CS = WTP - Price
  - On a graph: Consumer surplus is the area BELOW the demand curve and ABOVE the price line, from a quantity of 0 up to the quantity consumed (Q). For a straight-line demand curve, this forms a triangle, so you calculate it using the standard area of a triangle formula: 1/2 * base * height.

## Concept 2: Cost, Willingness to Sell, and Producer Surplus
- **Cost:** The value of everything a seller must give up to produce a good. It is a measure of their willingness to sell (they will only produce and sell if the market price is greater than their cost).
- **Producer Surplus (PS):** The amount a seller is paid for a good minus the seller's cost of providing it.
  - Formula: PS = Price - Cost
  - On a graph: Producer surplus is the area ABOVE the supply curve and BELOW the price line, from a quantity of 0 up to the quantity produced (Q). Like consumer surplus, for a straight-line supply curve, you use the area of a triangle formula: 1/2 * base * height.

## Concept 3: Total Surplus and Market Efficiency
- **Total Surplus (TS):** The total value to buyers minus the total cost to sellers.
  - Formula: TS = CS + PS
  - Also written as: TS = (Value to buyers) - (Cost to sellers)
- **Market Efficiency:** An allocation of resources is efficient if it maximizes Total Surplus.
  - Free markets naturally allocate the supply of goods to the buyers who value them most highly (those with the highest WTP).
  - Free markets naturally allocate the demand for goods to the sellers who can produce them at the lowest cost.
  - Free markets produce the exact quantity of goods that maximizes the sum of consumer and producer surplus (the equilibrium quantity).
  - *Conclusion:* Altering the market outcome (like using price controls or taxes) usually decreases total surplus and causes inefficiency (deadweight loss).

## Solved Question: Active Learning 1 (Calculating Consumer Surplus)
> **Question:** You are given a linear demand curve that goes from a price of 40 down to a price of 10 at quantity 30. The market price (P) is 30. 
> A. Find the marginal buyer's Willingness To Pay (WTP) at Q = 10.
> B. Find the Consumer Surplus (CS) for P = 30.
> Suppose P falls to 20. How much will CS increase due to...
> C. buyers entering the market?
> D. existing buyers paying a lower price?

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
- **A. Find marginal buyer's WTP at Q = 10:**
  Looking at the demand curve on a graph, at a quantity of 10, the height of the demand curve is 30. Therefore, the marginal buyer's WTP is **30 dollars**.
  
- **B. Find CS for P = 30:**
  The area for CS is a triangle above the price line (P=30) and below the demand curve. 
  - The base of the triangle is from Q=0 to Q=10. So base = 10.
  - The height of the triangle is the top of the demand curve minus the price: 40 - 30 = 10.
  - CS = 1/2 * base * height = 1/2 * 10 * 10 = **50 dollars**.
  
- **C. CS increase due to new buyers entering (P falls to 20):**
  When price falls to 20, quantity demanded increases from 10 to 20. The new buyers form a new small triangle.
  - The base is the new quantity minus the old quantity: 20 - 10 = 10 units.
  - The height is the old price minus the new price: 30 - 20 = 10 dollars.
  - Increase in CS from new buyers = 1/2 * 10 * 10 = **50 dollars**.
  
- **D. CS increase due to existing buyers paying less (P falls to 20):**
  The original 10 buyers used to pay 30, but now they pay 20. They save 10 dollars each. This forms a rectangle on the graph.
  - Increase in CS = 10 units * 10 dollars saved = **100 dollars**.
  *(Notice that total new CS = original 50 + new buyers 50 + existing buyers 100 = 200 dollars).*

## Solved Question: Active Learning 2 (Calculating Producer Surplus)
> **Question:** You are given a linear supply curve starting at the origin (0,0) and going up to P=45, Q=45. The market price (P) is 20.
> A. Find the marginal seller's cost at Q = 10.
> B. Find the total Producer Surplus (PS) for P = 20.
> Suppose P rises to 30. Find the increase in PS due to...
> C. selling 5 additional units?
> D. getting a higher price on the initial 10 units?

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
- **A. Find marginal seller's cost at Q = 10:**
  Looking at the supply curve on a graph, at a quantity of 10, the height of the supply curve is 20. Therefore, the marginal seller's cost is **20 dollars**.
  
- **B. Find total PS for P = 20:**
  The area for PS is a triangle below the price line (P=20) and above the supply curve.
  - The base of the triangle is from Q=0 to Q=10. So base = 10.
  - The height of the triangle is the price minus the bottom of the supply curve: 20 - 0 = 20.
  - PS = 1/2 * base * height = 1/2 * 10 * 20 = **100 dollars**.
  
- **C. PS increase due to selling 5 additional units (P rises to 30):**
  When price rises to 30, quantity supplied increases from 10 to 15. The new production forms a small triangle.
  - The base is the new quantity minus old quantity: 15 - 10 = 5 units.
  - The height is the new price minus the old price: 30 - 20 = 10 dollars.
  - Increase in PS from new units = 1/2 * 5 * 10 = **25 dollars**.
  
- **D. PS increase due to getting a higher price on initial 10 units (P rises to 30):**
  The original 10 units were sold at 20, but now they are sold at 30. The sellers make an extra 10 dollars on each unit. This forms a rectangle on the graph.
  - Increase in PS = 10 units * 10 dollars extra = **100 dollars**.
  *(Notice that total new PS = original 100 + new units 25 + existing units 100 = 225 dollars).*
